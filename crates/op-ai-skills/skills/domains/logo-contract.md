LOGO CONCEPT / REVIEW CONTRACT

This contract covers concept direction, critique, comparison and brand-board evidence. It does not promise production-grade curve engineering or a precision vector master. If an editable master is required, hand the approved concept to a dedicated vector-production pass.

## Route the job before drawing

- Existing mark, one candidate: critique it against the gates and tests below. Do not redesign it unless the user explicitly asks for a revision.
- Existing mark, multiple candidates: compare all candidates on the same evidence rows; do not let a more polished mockup change the criteria, and allow every candidate to be rejected.
- No mark: generate concepts only after the applicability gate passes.
- Prompt-only request: describe geometry and future proof states, not mood adjectives. State that no rendered artifact, visual QA or similarity screen was completed.

The unit of work is a recognizable relationship between name and meaning. A mascot, icon, monogram and wordmark placed side by side are a lockup, not automatically a fused idea.

## Applicability gate

Before concepting, obtain the exact name and the other three inputs below. If the name is unavailable, pause rather than inventing spelling or letterforms. Once the name is known, proceed with semantic fusion only when all four are true:

1. The exact name or letter sequence that must remain readable.
2. One secondary meaning that is essential to the product, behavior or promise.
3. A plausible piece of geometry that can carry both readings without explanation.
4. A real smallest-use context against which the geometry can be judged.

Otherwise take the conservative route. Apply exactly one of these exit gates, record the reason, and return a pure wordmark: correct spelling, proportion, spacing and weight, with no second pictorial reading.

- **EXIT 1 — no semantic necessity.** The second meaning is optional, invented from a mood board, or not central enough to survive product change.
- **EXIT 2 — no shared load-bearing geometry.** The readings remain two detachable objects, or one survives untouched when the other is removed.
- **EXIT 3 — primary reading or reduction fails.** Fusion changes the name, relies on a tiny detail, collapses at the declared minimum, or needs colour or prose to be understood.
- **EXIT 4 — trust or ownership risk.** The result creates an unsafe cultural reading, misstates the category, or becomes less distinctive by converging on a familiar mark.

Do not keep decorating after an exit. The fallback is the wordmark, not a weaker fusion.

## Eleven structural operations

These are geometry moves. Select one primary operation and at most one supporting operation; a list of aesthetic labels is not a concept.

1. **Boundary merge.** Make one outer edge close both the letterform and the secondary figure. Preserve the name's dominant silhouette; reject an outline that merely touches another icon.
2. **Path economy.** Route one continuous stroke through two readings, so changing its angle, weight or endpoint changes both. Decorative lines that only point at meaning do not qualify.
3. **Load-bearing replacement.** Replace one necessary glyph part with a semantically useful shape of the same structural role. Match width, stress and baseline before judging the metaphor.
4. **Counterform encoding.** Cut the secondary reading from an existing enclosed or inter-letter void. The void must remain open at the minimum size and must not puncture spelling.
5. **Orientation recast.** Rotate, mirror or crop an existing unit until its second reading appears while its first role remains identifiable. Do not add a second rotated copy as an annotation.
6. **Letterform reconstruction.** Build a glyph from parts native to the subject, then normalize those parts into a coherent alphabetic skeleton. Recognition of the letter wins over literal object detail.
7. **Language hinge.** Use a real overlap in sound, abbreviation, character component or meaning to decide which glyph carries the transformation. The relationship must work for the intended language audience without a caption.
8. **Behavior distillation.** Reduce a product action or service sequence to its decisive spatial relation, then embed that relation in the wordmark. Represent the cause-and-effect, not a miniature interface scene.
9. **Mother-form reuse.** Begin with one repeated or radial whole, then crop, stripe, subtract or locally deform only parts already present. The altered parts may reveal the second reading, but the complete group must retain its first silhouette; adding a new motif is not reuse.
10. **Bridge-form reduction.** When the two readings share no direct geometry, use a third adjacent object or action only to discover a compatible silhouette. Remove that scaffold from the final mark: the bridge passes only when it reduces the object count instead of becoming a connector or third icon.
11. **Module-and-void tuning.** Rebalance repeated modules, gaps and the exterior contour until the second reading emerges from the whole. Judge the filled silhouette and the empty intervals together; never hide the idea in one micro-detail.

For every concept, state: primary operation, shared geometric carrier, preserved name cue, secondary meaning, and expected failure point. If those five fields cannot be filled without adjectives, the concept is not yet structural.

## Fusion tests — all four must pass

- **F1 — double-duty carrier.** Point to one contour, path, void or module that performs a necessary job in both readings.
- **F2 — counterfactual edit.** Try to erase either reading; passing requires changing the same load-bearing geometry, not deleting a detachable badge.
- **F3 — single visual body.** At a glance the result has one silhouette and one rhythm, rather than a word plus an attached illustration.
- **F4 — earned discovery.** The secondary reading emerges from inspecting the primary form; it is not announced by an extra arrow, caption, outline or colour patch.

One failure means the fusion claim is rejected. Do not average the four results into a score.

## Rendering predicates

- **R1 — monochrome proof first.** Before selecting palette, render solid black on white and the exact reverse. Both versions must preserve spelling, the shared carrier and one-body silhouette.
- **R2 — minimum-size proof.** Declare the smallest intended use, render at its actual pixel dimensions, and inspect that output rather than a zoomed canvas. Essential counters stay open, distinct strokes do not merge, and the secondary reading remains available without squinting.
- **R3 — colour comes after proof.** Add colour only after R1 and R2 pass. Turning colour off must not alter any fusion-test verdict; colour may organize emphasis but cannot carry required meaning.

Review the rendered artifact, not only source paths. A concept board must show the lockup, construction explanation, both monochrome states and the actual minimum-size sample. A comparison or QA board must expose one evidence cell per fusion test and mark pass/fail with a reason. Untested cells remain `not run`; an empty cell is never evidence of a pass.

PASS ONLY WHEN all four fusion tests and all three rendering predicates survive in the exported artifact; otherwise return the pure wordmark.
